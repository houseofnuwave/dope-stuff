# Founder pilot playbook · Test revenue without damaging trust

## Target customer

Reseller in the U.S. with ~20–500 unique pieces (designer, contemporary, streetwear, second-hand), using at least two resale channels. They already feel friction from repeated listing entry, keeping track of status, and manually identifying sold pieces still listed elsewhere. Exclude sellers expecting prohibited automation, counterfeit sales or algorithmic “guaranteed sales.”

## Narrow promise

> Keep your inventory organized, prepare listings quickly, know where your items are listed, and see your recorded profit. Assisted publishing today; no automatic marketplace posting or syncing.

A trustworthy narrow promise is commercially stronger than claiming universal automation that cannot be maintained.

## First 30 days — proposed, not already conducted

**Days 1–7: 10 discovery calls.** Ask sellers to show the last item they listed, how long it took, their spreadsheet/process, how they handled their last sold item across multiple channels, and what they pay for tools today. Record workflows and problems, not just stated feature preferences.

**Days 8–14: 5 moderated usability trials.** Ask participants to enter 3 real items, prepare a draft, mark a manual post, record a fictional *test transaction* clearly labeled as such, and find the removal queue. Measure time, errors, confusion, and perceived trust; do not put fictitious transactions into users' real books.

**Days 15–21: 15–25 closed pilot sellers.** Require informed consent, a support channel, secure hosting and the launch gates appropriate to a private pilot. Invite the users to work with actual inventory, capture consented usage analytics, and personally assist onboarding.

**Days 22–30: pricing validation.** Offer a proposed $14.99 Starter and $39.99 Pro concept with unambiguous feature lists. Ask for a real commitment (e.g. request checkout access for a pilot *when payment infrastructure and deliverables are ready*). Only charge after vetted billing/terms/cancellation and sufficient delivered value; don't fake conversions.

## Beta success criteria (hypotheses to test)

- At least 60% of qualified testers create 3 real records within 48h.
- Median item preparation time is 30% faster than *that user's* baseline for an equivalent listing.
- At least half of activated testers return to perform a meaningful workflow the next week.
- Fewer than 5% of testers misunderstand the manual marketplace status after onboarding.
- At least 20% of qualified engaged sellers express credible willingness to buy the correctly described subscription. This is not the same as actual paid conversion.
- Zero confirmed cross-account data exposures or false “automatically removed” incidents.

Adjust thresholds based on evidence; don't cherry-pick a positive outcome.

## Experiment dashboard

| Experiment | Evidence to gather | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Value of listing preparation | Time before vs after, preferred channels | Improve kit / stop if no gain |
| Inventory protection | Number and age of manual-delisting tasks | Build reminders when lawful |
| Profit dashboard | How often sellers consult it and correct cost inputs | Keep / simplify |
| Subscription interest | Actual paid intent and feature expectations | Validate Starter/Pro price |
| Import/export | Time for seller with 100 SKUs to onboard | Prioritize better importer |

## Economic model (illustrative only)

At $39.99 monthly and hypothetical 75% gross margin, one subscriber yields ~$30/month contribution *before* acquisition cost and fixed overhead. At 4% monthly churn, simplistic steady-state gross-profit LTV is ~$750. Real churn, payment fees, support burden, platform disruption and cohort mix must replace those assumptions. An apparent cheaper price can destroy margins if every customer needs constant manual support.

## 80/20 roadmap

Spend the first 80% of engineering effort on: (1) accurate canonical inventory, (2) excellent manual listing kits, (3) trustworthy sold-item removal tasks, and (4) smooth onboarding with a feedback loop. Defer trendy AI features, native apps, unsupported marketplaces and automatic repricing until evidence supports them.

## Commercial and brand critical review

- **Founder assumption to challenge:** “All five competitors combined” may be a more expensive product than customers need. Users buy saved time and fewer errors, not a feature count.
- **Engineering assumption to challenge:** More channels add cost and risk. Every integration is a support liability and possible platform-policy dependency.
- **UX assumption to challenge:** A beautiful interface alone does not increase lifetime value. Reliability, control, data portability and explainable status do.
- **Alternative:** If a multi-platform integration cannot be approved, target a narrower studio operations niche with the best inventory, seller checklist and profitability tool rather than chasing unauthorized automation.
- **Long-range direction:** Year 1 = prove a retention-worthy workflow; years 2–3 = approved integrations and profitable cohorts; years 3–5 = multi-user resale operations if customers pull that way; years 5–10 = trusted commerce operations platform only if partner economics and brand trust support it. None of these outcomes is guaranteed.
